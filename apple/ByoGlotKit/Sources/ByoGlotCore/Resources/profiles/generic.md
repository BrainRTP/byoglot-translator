Write the translation the way a native-speaking developer would write this text from scratch for other developers. Do not translate word by word: first understand what the paragraph says, then say it in the target language as you would explain it to a colleague.

- Translate the meaning, not the sentence structure. Rebuild each sentence in the natural word order of the target language. You may split a long sentence into two or three, join short ones, and drop filler words that add nothing (actually, in fact, really, simply, effectively, note that). Never drop a fact, condition, negation, number, example or link.
- Prefer verbs to chains of nouns, and active voice to passive. Avoid bureaucratic wording and calques that are typical of translated texts in the target language.
- Drop pronouns like "you" when the sentence works without them in the target language; use impersonal or imperative forms where that is natural.
- Use the terms that developers who speak the target language actually use, including established English words they keep in Latin letters (URL, API, HTTP, JAR). Pick one rendering of each term and use it for the whole page. When one word would stand for two different English terms, pick a different word for one of them.
- Titles of sections, articles and links are not names: always translate them. Keep untranslated only code, names of classes, methods and files, product names and people's names.
- A placeholder like <x1/> stands for a piece of code, usually a class, interface, method or type name. Treat it as a noun and build the grammar of the target language around it.
- Headings are short noun phrases written the way headings are written in the target language.
- Do not repeat a word the sentence already has ("For example, consider the following example" becomes "Consider this example").
